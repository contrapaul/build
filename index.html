Habit: be specific

Name it. Describe it. Say what done looks like.

Make the video bigger and nicer.VAGUE
The video should load first in a player that fills ~90+% of either horizontal or vertical dimensions, depending on video size. Once it ends I’d like it to dissolve/fade... and have the question load in, filling much of the screen but leaving breathing room.SPECIFIC: MY TRIVIA PROJECT

Habit: write the rules down

Class rules, for the agent.

Every rule says why. Same mistake twice, and it becomes a rule.

trivia/CLAUDE.md, a rules file
Nothing is fetched from another host, ever. Fonts are in public/fonts. No CDN, no Google Fonts. Those are unreachable in China and a blocking request to a dead host stops the page in front of a room full of people.

Do not add questions. TBD in the data means Paul has not settled it.

Legible from the back of the room. Every screen is judged at a distance. Bigger is better. Nothing depends on hover.

A rule I hold myself to: no AI art

An AI poster, warning you about AI.

  1. Slogans that come in threes
  2. “In today’s fast-paced world”
  3. An em dash, in the advice on how to spot AI writing
  4. A QR code that goes nowhere, on a poster about fake sources

Students notice.Students know.Students judge.

The other side

Tell students what AI made, and why.

When they see how it was used, and what it made possible, they buy in. We ask students for AI disclosure. We should model it.

Start here

Your first site, live.

A one-page website at your own address, built with an AI coding agent. About an hour the first time. Scroll, or use the arrow keys.

01

What you need

  • A GitHub account. Free.
  • A Cloudflare account. Free.
  • An AI coding agent: Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, GitHub Copilot, Cursor or Gemini CLI. Most need a paid plan. Any agentic one will do.
  • Optional: your own domain, about the price of a coffee a year.
02

Pick something you need

Not something impressive. Need makes you specific.

  • A quiz for one unit, with an explanation for each wrong answer
  • A sorting activity: drag terms into groups
  • A countdown to an exam or event
  • A helper for writing report comments from your own phrases
03

Write a one-page brief

Before any code, answer four questions in plain language. That is your brief.

  • Who uses it?
  • Where? A projector, a phone, a school laptop. Say which screen.
  • What does done look like? “A student on a phone can finish it without scrolling sideways.”
  • What must it never do? “Ask for names.” “Load anything from another website.”

A template is at the end of this page.

04

Build it in the browser

The easiest first route: nothing to install.

  1. On GitHub, create a new, empty repo.
  2. Open a cloud session on it (for example, claude.ai/code).
  3. Paste your brief and ask: “Write a plan for this. Don’t write code yet.”
  4. Read the plan. Change what is wrong. Approve it.
  5. Ask it to build, and to add a CLAUDE.md and a HANDOFF.md using the templates below.
  6. Review what it made, then merge it into your main branch.

On your own computer instead? Install your agent, clone the repo, and follow the same steps in a local session, with a live preview.

05

Publish it

  1. In Cloudflare: Workers & Pages, Create, Pages, Connect to Git.
  2. Choose your repo.
  3. Leave the build settings blank. A static site needs none.
  4. Save and Deploy. You get an address within a minute.
  5. Optional: Custom domains, then add a subdomain of your own domain.

From now on, every push goes live by itself.

06

Make it yours

  • One change at a time. Check it yourself.
  • When it works, commit.
  • Don’t know what something is called? Ask. Then use that word.
  • Same mistake twice? Add a rule to your rules file.
  • Something wrong? Read the thinking to see what it misunderstood.
07

Before you share it

  • No student data: no names, no accounts, no tracking.
  • Add a license, so people know what they may reuse.
  • Add an AI disclosure line: what AI made, what you did.
  • No AI images. Use your own, your students’ (with credit), or none.
  • Open it on a phone.

Working from mainland China

  • Most AI coding tools need a VPN.
  • GitHub works, but can be slow.
  • Cloudflare Pages on your own domain loads normally. *.pages.dev and github.io addresses often don’t.
  • Keep everything in the project: no Google Fonts, no CDNs.
08

Starter files

Copy these into your repo. They work with any agent. Some agents look for AGENTS.md instead of CLAUDE.md: check which yours reads.

BRIEF.md
# Brief: [name]

## Who uses it
[Students in grade X / colleagues / me on a projector]

## Where
[Projector at 16:9 / phones / school laptops]

## What it does
[Two or three sentences]

## Done looks like
- [A student can ... without ...]
- [It loads in under ... on school wifi]

## Must never
- Ask for names or accounts
- Load anything from another website
HANDOFF.md
# Handoff

Newest entry at the top. The agent updates this after every working change.

## Status
[One paragraph: what works, what does not yet]

## Open items
- [ ]

## Gotchas that have already bitten

## Log
### [date]: [what changed]
CLAUDE.md (or AGENTS.md)
# Rules for this project

Read BRIEF.md and HANDOFF.md before changing anything.

- No student data. No names, no accounts, no tracking. Why: this is public.
- Nothing is loaded from another website. Fonts and scripts are saved in
  the project. Why: blocked sites stop the page loading.
- One change at a time, and say what you changed. Why: I need to check it.
- Ask before guessing. If the brief does not say, ask me.
- After every change that works, add a dated entry to the top of
  HANDOFF.md: what was asked, what was built, what was decided, and what
  is still unchecked. Do this before telling me you are finished.
